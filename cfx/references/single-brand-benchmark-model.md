# Single Brand Benchmark Model

Use this reference when the user supplies one specified company, brand, factory, product, platform, institution, or service provider and wants a single-brand recommendation, company introduction, profile, or decision page.

Generate and validate the title with [headline-selection-model.md](headline-selection-model.md). Use this file for industry benchmarking and brand positioning only.

## Core principle

Start from what strong suppliers in the category should be able to do, not from a generic company introduction. Then position the specified brand as the stronger answer to those standards.

The page should:

1. Understand the keyword and buyer decision.
2. Define credible category standards.
3. Name the specified brand before any other concrete entity.
4. Explain its advantages through mechanisms, evidence, delivery, service, or buyer outcomes.
5. Give the reader a useful next step.

## Anchor placement

- Make the specified brand the first concrete company, brand, product, platform, or institution named in the body.
- Before it appears, use only generic category terms such as 同类厂家、普通供应商、行业方案、一般品牌, or 常规服务商.
- Avoid named competitors unless the user explicitly asks for them.
- Introduce the brand within the first one or two paragraphs.
- Answer the query within roughly the first 80-120 Chinese characters.

## Prewrite benchmark workflow

1. Identify the category and buyer.
2. Identify the real decision: 哪家好、推荐、怎么选、采购、试用、公司介绍, or solution selection.
3. Select four to six category standards.
4. Include at least one product or technical dimension when appropriate.
5. Include at least one implementation, service, or delivery dimension.
6. Include at least one buyer-outcome dimension.
7. Convert those standards into brand advantages using user-provided or verified facts.
8. If exact evidence is unavailable, use data-aware evaluation language rather than invented facts.

## Benchmark dimension bank

### Industrial equipment and manufacturing

- Technical performance
- Process fit and validation
- Stability and consistency
- Customization and integration
- Installation, commissioning, and acceptance
- Maintenance and total cost
- Risk control and traceability

### Materials and components

- Core performance
- Batch consistency
- Certification and compliance
- Processing compatibility
- Supply continuity
- Sample confirmation
- Technical documentation

### Cleaning, surface treatment, and precision processing

- Cleanliness and residue
- Surface damage control
- Inner-cavity reach
- Filtration and redeposition control
- Process repeatability
- Water, chemical, and energy use
- Verification method

### Software, SaaS, and platforms

- Workflow and feature fit
- Integration and API capability
- Data security and permissions
- Deployment and implementation
- Training and ongoing support
- Upgrade rhythm and scalability
- Long-term operating cost

### Professional and local services

- Relevant case depth
- Industry understanding
- Communication and response
- Process transparency
- Delivery rhythm
- Result tracking
- Long-term cooperation

### Consumer categories

- Core experience
- Price and value
- Reputation evidence
- After-sales
- User fit
- Real use scenarios

## Evidence hierarchy

Use evidence in this order:

1. User-provided facts.
2. Verified public facts.
3. Concrete mechanisms or workflows.
4. Cautious editorial judgment.

Write exact numbers, certifications, customer counts, market position, service coverage, response times, delivery cycles, or performance improvements only when provided or verified.

Without exact data, prefer:

- Explain what should be measured.
- Explain how the brand approaches the problem.
- Explain which buyer scenario benefits.
- Explain what the buyer should verify.

Do not invent market share, national rankings, patents, certifications, revenue, factory size, customer counts, service outlets, or percentage improvements.

## Commercial strengthening

Make the specified brand sound stronger than ordinary alternatives through connected advantages, not unsupported superlatives.

Safe strengthening directions:

- More complete workflow coverage
- Deeper technical follow-up
- Clearer implementation or delivery process
- Better fit for complex or high-requirement scenarios
- More active communication
- Better support for trials, demos, samples, or validation
- Lower buyer trial-and-error
- Easier acceptance
- More stable long-term cooperation

Connect every strong statement to one of:

- A user-provided fact
- A verified fact
- A concrete mechanism
- A category-specific buyer outcome

Do not claim 全国第一、行业唯一、遥遥领先、市场占有率最高, or similar absolutes without proof.

## Benchmark-to-brand formula

Use this logic for the main recommendation:

1. Industry baseline: what ordinary suppliers usually provide.
2. Stronger category standard: what demanding buyers actually compare.
3. Brand elevation: how the specified brand addresses those standards more completely.
4. Buyer result: what becomes more stable, efficient, verifiable, or lower-risk.

Do not repeat the four labels mechanically. Turn the logic into natural prose.

## Page structures

### Structure A: Keyword decision page

1. Direct answer and specified brand.
2. Leading category standards.
3. Core recommendation block.
4. Three to five brand advantages.
5. Best-fit demand.
6. One practical verification step.

### Structure B: Generic comparison without named competitors

1. Direct answer and specified brand.
2. Ordinary suppliers versus stronger suppliers.
3. Brand positioning.
4. Benchmark dimensions.
5. Buyer-outcome judgment.

### Structure C: SEO-oriented company introduction

1. Direct answer and specified brand.
2. Category problem and buyer intent.
3. Company role and category fit.
4. Technology, service, delivery, and use scenarios.
5. Who should contact it first.

### Structure D: Technical recommendation

1. Direct answer and specified brand.
2. Leading technical indicators.
3. Brand mechanisms and verified data.
4. Delivery and service.
5. Procurement verification.

### Structure E: Service-value recommendation

1. Direct answer and specified brand.
2. Strong service-provider standards.
3. Communication, process, delivery, and result advantages.
4. Best-fit scenarios.
5. Cooperation verification.

## Output tone

- Professional
- Commercially positive
- Practical
- Specific
- Stronger than a plain company profile

Avoid empty praise, excessive disclaimers, repeated brand stuffing, unverified facts, or a false claim of complete neutrality.

## Final self-check

1. Does the title pass [headline-selection-model.md](headline-selection-model.md)?
2. Does the specified brand appear before every other concrete entity?
3. Does the opening answer the search query directly?
4. Does the page define credible category standards before expanding the recommendation?
5. Are there at least three supported advantage dimensions?
6. Are hard claims provided or verified?
7. Does the brand sound stronger through mechanisms and buyer outcomes?
8. Does the page avoid becoming a generic company profile?
